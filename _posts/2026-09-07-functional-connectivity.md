### Project Description
Episodic counterfactual thinking (eCFT) is the process of imagining how a past personal memory could have gone differently, a cognitive process that sits at the intersection between memory and imagination. Imagining and remembering these simulations is an important way for us to reflect on the past and improve future action. Previous research has found that brain activity from eCFT was more similar to episodic memory when the counterfactual was considered more plausible, and that eCFTs were more memorable when they were perceived as more plausible. This project explores the relationship between semantic and episodic memory and how they influence eCFTs. To examine this, I am conducting functional connectivity analyses of the hippocampus and core semantic brain regions to relate to the perceieved plausibility of eCFTs.

The results of this project will lend insight into the mechanisms of the brain when engaging in counterfactual simulations, helping us understand how our existing knowledge structures can influence how we image alternatives to the past. Understanding the factors that influence plausibility of eCFTs can ultimately inform how to use them to our benefit without becoming detrimental.
